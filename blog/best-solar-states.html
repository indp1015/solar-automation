Best States for Solar ROI in 2026 (Ranked)

Solar economics vary wildly across the U.S. The best states combine high electricity rates, strong sun, and favorable net metering. Here's our 2026 ranking based on combined payback and 25-year ROI for a standard 6kW system.

Top 10 states for solar ROI (2026)

  1. Hawaii — 40¢/kWh rates + generous state credit. Payback 4-5 years despite 50% export credit.
  2. Massachusetts — 30¢/kWh + 1:1 net metering + SMART incentive. Payback 5-6 years.
  3. California — 31¢/kWh + strong sun. Payback 7-9 years after NEM 3.0.
  4. Rhode Island — 29¢/kWh + 1:1 net metering. Payback 5-6 years.
  5. Connecticut — 29¢/kWh + strong incentives. Payback 5-7 years.
  6. New York — 26¢/kWh + VDER + state credit. Payback 7-9 years.
  7. New Jersey — 22¢/kWh + 1:1 net metering + SREC-II. Payback 7-8 years.
  8. Arizona — 14¢/kWh but exceptional sun + state credit. Payback 6-8 years.
  9. New Mexico — 14¢/kWh + strong sun + 10% state credit. Payback 7-9 years.
  10. Colorado — 14.5¢/kWh + 1:1 net metering. Payback 7-9 years.

States where solar payback is slowest

  1. Louisiana — 12.5¢/kWh + moderate sun + limited incentives. Payback 15-18 years.
  2. Alabama — 16¢/kWh but no net metering and no state incentive. Payback 12-15 years.
  3. South Dakota — 13¢/kWh + weak sun in north. Payback 14-16 years.
  4. North Dakota — 12¢/kWh + coldest climate. Payback 14-17 years.
  5. Mississippi — 12¢/kWh + limited state support. Payback 13-16 years.

These states aren't solar-hostile — they simply have cheap grid power and limited incentives, so payback takes longer.

What drives the rankings

Three factors determine payback:

  • Electricity rate — the higher your current bill, the more solar saves per kWh
  • Solar resource — measured in peak sun hours per day (2.9 in Seattle, 6.5 in Phoenix)
  • Net metering policy — 1:1 vs reduced credit changes ROI by 30-80%

A state with 30¢ rates, 4.5 peak sun hours, and 1:1 net metering (Massachusetts) often beats a state with 15¢ rates, 6 peak sun hours, and reduced credit (Arizona), despite Arizona's superior sun.

What matters more than your state

Within-state variation is significant. In California, payback ranges from 7.9 years (Santa Barbara) to 11.0 years (Humboldt). In Texas, from 9.4 years (El Paso) to 11.5 years (Wichita). Always check your specific county — our interactive pages show the exact payback for your location.

Bottom line

If you're in the top 10 solar states, expect payback of 5-9 years. In the bottom 5, consider waiting for better incentives, expanding system size, or exploring community solar options that don't require roof installation.
