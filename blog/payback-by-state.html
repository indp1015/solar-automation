Solar Payback Period by State: 2026 Comparison

Solar payback — the years until cumulative savings equal your net system cost — ranges from under 5 years in Hawaii to over 17 in parts of the Deep South. Here's the 2026 landscape.

Fastest payback states (under 7 years)

  • Hawaii: 4-5 years (40¢ rates + state credit)
  • Massachusetts: 5-6 years (30¢ rates + SMART)
  • Rhode Island: 5-6 years (29¢ rates + 1:1 NEM)
  • Connecticut: 5-7 years (29¢ rates + strong state incentives)
  • California: 7-9 years (31¢ rates, offset by NEM 3.0)

Medium payback states (8-12 years)

  • New York, New Jersey: 7-9 years
  • Arizona, New Mexico: 7-9 years (great sun, moderate rates)
  • Colorado, Nevada: 8-10 years
  • Oregon, Washington: 10-12 years (lower sun, low rates)

Slow payback states (13-17 years)

  • Louisiana: 15-18 years
  • Alabama, Mississippi: 13-16 years
  • South Dakota, North Dakota: 14-17 years
  • Oklahoma, Arkansas: 12-15 years

What drives the differences

Three factors explain 90% of state variation:

1. Electricity rate (biggest lever)

At 30¢/kWh, solar saves $1,500/year on 5,000 kWh of self-consumption. At 12¢/kWh, the same production saves $600. Payback differs by 2.5x for the same system.

2. Solar resource

Phoenix gets 5.5 peak sun hours/day; Seattle gets 3.1. That's a 77% production difference — enough to shift payback by 2-3 years, all else equal.

3. Policy (net metering + state incentives)

1:1 net metering doubles the value of excess generation vs NEM 3.0. State tax credits (NY 25%, MA 15%, HI 35%) reduce net cost by another $1,000-$3,000.

Within-state variation is 3-4 years

Texas alone spans 9.4-11.5 years across 254 counties. California spans 7.9-11.0 across 58. The specific county matters as much as the state — check our county pages for exact numbers.

How to estimate your own payback

Quick formula:

Payback = Net cost after ITC ÷ (Annual production × your rate)

Example: 6kW in Atlanta (1,300 kWh/kW/yr, 14¢ rate):

  • Net cost: $12,390 (after 30% ITC)
  • Annual savings: 6 × 1,300 × $0.14 = $1,092
  • Payback: 11.3 years (before escalation)

With 2.5% annual rate escalation, actual payback drops to ~10.5 years — the savings grow over time, so cumulative reaches net cost faster.

Why payback alone isn't enough

Payback ignores what happens after — a system with 15-year payback and 25-year life still delivers 10 years of free electricity, worth $10,000-$30,000. IRR captures this better: our calculated IRRs range from 5% (slow states) to 20%+ (fast states). Compare IRR to your opportunity cost (bond yields, mortgage prepayment, etc.).

Bottom line

Payback 5-7 years = excellent (top states). 8-12 years = solid. 13+ years = consider waiting or exploring community solar. But always check your specific county and run the numbers with current policy — state averages hide 3-4 years of intra-state variation.
