2026 Federal Solar Tax Credit (ITC) — Complete Guide

The federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC) is the single most valuable incentive for residential solar in the United States. For systems installed between 2023 and 2032, it covers 30% of your total system cost as a dollar-for-dollar reduction in federal income tax owed.

How the 30% credit works

If you install a $20,000 solar system, the ITC reduces your federal tax bill by $6,000. This is not a deduction — it is a direct credit. If your tax liability is less than $6,000, the unused portion carries forward to future tax years.

What's covered by the credit

The ITC applies to the full installed cost, including: solar panels, inverters, mounting hardware, wiring, labor, permits, and (for systems installed 2023+) battery storage paired with solar.

Who qualifies

You must own the system (not lease it) and have federal tax liability. If you lease or use a Power Purchase Agreement (PPA), the third-party owner claims the ITC and passes savings to you via lower monthly payments.

How to claim

File IRS Form 5695 with your federal tax return. Your installer should provide a detailed invoice showing the full system cost. Keep the documentation — the IRS occasionally audits ITC claims.

Interaction with state incentives

The ITC is calculated on gross cost before state rebates. So if you receive a $2,000 state rebate, your ITC is still 30% of the original $20,000 = $6,000. Total savings stack rather than cancel.

Sunset timeline

The 30% rate holds through 2032. In 2033 it drops to 26%, 2034 to 22%, and expires for residential installations in 2035 (commercial continues at 10%).
