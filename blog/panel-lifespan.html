How Long Do Solar Panels Really Last? (2026 Data)

Solar panels are typically warranted for 25-30 years of power production, but real-world lifespan is more nuanced. Here's what NREL long-term studies and installer field data actually show.

Panel degradation: 0.5%/year

Modern Tier-1 panels degrade at roughly 0.5% per year. After 25 years, they retain ~88% of original output. This is the industry-standard linear warranty, and field data from NREL's long-term studies largely confirms it.

Older panels (pre-2010) degraded faster, around 1%/year. The improvement comes from better passivation layers and lower defect rates in monocrystalline cells.

What actually fails first: the inverter

While panels last 30+ years, string inverters typically fail at 10-15 years. Replacement costs $1,500-$2,500 for a residential system. Our ROI calculator includes a $1,800 inverter replacement in year 13 to reflect this real-world cost.

Microinverters (Enphase) and DC optimizers (SolarEdge) often have 25-year warranties, but their upfront cost is higher. For most homeowners, string inverter + mid-life replacement is still the better economic choice.

What about the roof?

Panels don't determine system lifespan — the roof does. Asphalt shingles last 20-25 years, so it's usually smart to replace your roof before or during solar installation if it's already 15+ years old. This adds $5,000-$15,000 but avoids costly removal-and-reinstall at year 20.

Real-world data (NREL, 2024)

A 2024 NREL analysis of 6,000+ residential systems in the U.S. found:

  • Median measured degradation: 0.54%/year
  • Systems still producing 80%+ of original output at year 20: 94%
  • Median inverter replacement: year 12.7

Bottom line

Expect 30+ year panel life, one inverter replacement around year 13, and total system economics that remain cash-flow positive through year 25 in almost all U.S. markets.
