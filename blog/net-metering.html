Net Metering (NEM 3.0) Explained: How It Affects Your Savings

Net metering is the mechanism that credits you for solar electricity you send back to the grid. The policy varies dramatically across states, and it is the single biggest variable in solar ROI after installation cost.

Three main net metering structures

1. Full 1:1 retail credit

Every kWh you export earns the same rate you pay for grid electricity. This is the most favorable structure. Currently mandated in approximately 30 states including Florida, New York (for systems under 10kW), and Colorado.

2. Reduced export credit

Exports earn a lower avoided-cost rate. Examples: California's NEM 3.0 (~25% of retail), Arizona utilities (~40-70% of retail), Hawaii's CGS+ (~50%). Payback periods lengthen by 30-80% versus 1:1.

3. No statewide net metering

Texas and a few other states have no mandate. Some utilities voluntarily offer buyback at low rates. Self-consumption is critical — battery storage becomes more valuable.

Self-consumption vs export

In any net metering structure, electricity you use on-site is worth the full retail rate. Only excess (exported) generation is subject to the export rate. This is why sizing systems to annual usage — not oversized — usually delivers the best ROI.

NEM 3.0's impact in California

Before NEM 3.0 (April 2023), a typical California system had a 6-8 year payback. After NEM 3.0, paybacks for the same system extend to 9-13 years without battery storage. Adding a battery to shift exports to evening peak hours recovers much of the lost value.

What to check before installing

Confirm your utility's current net metering policy — it may differ from the state mandate. Grandfathering periods exist: systems installed before major policy changes often keep the old rates for 15-20 years.
