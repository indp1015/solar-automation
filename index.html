Current U.S. Solar Market Insights

Navigating the solar market requires understanding the balance between installation costs, local electricity rates, and available incentives. In today's dynamic environment, homeowners must look beyond the sticker price to grasp the full financial picture.

1. The Impact of Electricity Rates on ROI

Your solar payback period is heavily dictated by how much you currently pay for electricity. States with high rates (like California and New York) often see significantly faster payback periods compared to states with cheap grid power. Our Calculator uses precise EIA data to model your specific situation.

2. Solar Radiation and Production

While sunshine is abundant across the U.S., actual solar production varies by region. We utilize PVGIS (NREL) data to accurately estimate the kWh output of a system based on your specific county's latitude and longitude. (Note: US radiation data is a 10-year average from 2005-2015).

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

How is the payback period calculated?

We calculate net cost after the 30% federal ITC, then accumulate annual savings year-by-year (accounting for 2.5% rate escalation and 0.5% panel degradation) until the cumulative savings equal the net cost.

Why does the solar radiation data date back to 2015?

Our data provider (PVGIS) uses a 10-year average from the National Solar Radiation Database (NSRDB) covering 2005-2015. This provides a reliable long-term baseline for estimating system performance.

Does this include state or local incentives?

Currently, our calculations factor in only the 30% federal Investment Tax Credit (ITC). State and local incentives (DSIRE) are provided for reference; consult a local installer for specific financial advice.
