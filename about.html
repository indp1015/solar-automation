About Solar Data Hub

Our mission is to provide transparent, data-driven estimates for solar panel investments across the United States.

About This Site

What this site is: Solar Data Hub is an independent, data-driven resource for U.S. homeowners, researchers, and solar installers. We aggregate public data from the U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA), the National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL), PVGIS, and DSIRE to provide county-level solar ROI estimates, electricity rates, and policy summaries — all in one place, free of charge.

Who operates it: This site is operated by an independent data analyst based in South Korea. We are not affiliated with any solar installer, utility company, or government agency. Our goal is to make public energy data easier to understand and compare across all 3,100+ U.S. counties.

Why it exists: Solar payback periods vary dramatically by location — from under 6 years in Hawaii to over 20 years in parts of the Midwest. Most homeowners don't have the time to cross-reference EIA rate tables, NREL solar radiation maps, and state net-metering rules. We do that work and present it in a single, comparable format.

How we make money: Solar Data Hub is supported by display advertising (Google AdSense). We do not sell user data, and we do not accept payment from installers to rank their services. Advertisements are clearly distinguished from editorial content.

Contact: Questions, corrections, or partnership inquiries: contact@dataindp.com

Our Assumptions (and Why)

System cost: $2.70/W + $1500 fixed install cost. Based on the U.S. median installed cost for residential solar in 2024–2025 (NREL Q1 2025 benchmark: $2.50–$2.90/W before incentives), plus a fixed cost for permits, labor setup, and interconnection.

Federal ITC: 30%. The Inflation Reduction Act extended the 30% residential tax credit through 2032. We assume the full credit is usable in year 1; if you have no federal tax liability, a carry-forward or reduced benefit applies.

Electricity rate escalation: 2.5% per year. The EIA Annual Energy Outlook projects 2–3% long-term residential escalation. We use 2.5% as a conservative midpoint.

Panel degradation: 0.5% per year. Matches the median of Tier-1 manufacturer warranties (0.5%/yr linear) and NREL long-term field studies.

Operations & maintenance: 1.0% of gross system cost per year. Covers cleaning, monitoring, and minor repairs. Inverter replacement (~$300/kW, so $1800 for a 6kW system) is modeled in year 13.

Net metering: Our baseline assumes 1:1 retail-rate credit for exported production. See the state-specific notes below — two states are modeled differently due to policy changes.

Discount rate for NPV: 5%. Reflects the opportunity cost of capital at current Treasury yields.

⚠️ State-Specific Net Metering Assumptions

Net metering rules vary by state and utility. Our baseline assumes 1:1 retail-rate credit for exported solar production. Two states are modeled differently based on current policy:

  • California (NEM 3.0, since April 2023): export credit ≈ 25% of retail rate. Payback periods are correspondingly longer.
  • Hawaii (CGS+/NEM+): export credit ≈ 50% of retail rate.

Other states use 1:1 retail credit as the default. Actual credits depend on your utility, interconnection date, and system size — consult your installer.

🔧 Inverter Replacement

Standard string inverters have a useful life of 10–15 years. We model a $300/kW inverter replacement in year 13 ($1800 for a 6kW system). This reduces the 25-year ROI by 5–15 percentage points.

ⓘ Note: Because payback periods in most states occur before year 13, the inverter replacement does not extend payback — it only reduces total 25-year ROI. Microinverter systems (e.g., Enphase) typically avoid this mid-life cost.
